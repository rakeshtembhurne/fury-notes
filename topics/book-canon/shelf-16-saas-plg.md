# SaaS / B2B Marketing Playbooks — PLG, Onboarding, Activation, Expansion, Customer Success

Shelf: SaaS/B2B marketing playbooks for a technical founder running SaaS + e-commerce.
Format per book: title, author, year, one-line thesis, 3 actionable tactics.
Verification: Open Library API (openlibrary.org/search.json) unless noted.

---

## 1. Product-Led Growth — Wes Bush — 2019 ✅ OL
**Thesis:** Make the product itself the primary acquisition, conversion, and expansion engine instead of sales-led funnels.
- Launch a free trial/freemium motion gated on value moments (aha → habit), not on time alone.
- Instrument a PQL (product-qualified lead) score from usage signals and route hot accounts to sales/assists.
- Design pricing tiers around value metrics (seats, usage, outcomes) so growth auto-expands revenue.

## 2. Product-Led Onboarding — Ramli John (with Wes Bush) — 2021 ✅ Google Books / Amazon (self-published via ProductLed Press; absent from Open Library)
**Thesis:** Onboarding is a 6-step EUREKA system that turns new signups into lifelong customers by engineering the first impression.
- Map the user's desired outcome first, then work backward to the shortest path to value.
- Match value moments to user behaviors and use checklists/progress to pull users forward.
- Loop sales and high-touch support into onboarding for high-value accounts instead of pure self-serve.

## 3. Hooked — Nir Eyal (with Ryan Hoover) — 2014 ✅ OL
**Thesis:** Habit-forming products are built with the Hook loop: trigger → action → variable reward → investment.
- Attach your product to existing internal triggers (boredom, anxiety, FOMO) rather than manufacturing new ones.
- Reduce every core action to the fewest possible steps so ability never blocks motivation.
- Ask for stored value (data, content, followers, preferences) so each visit makes the next more likely.

## 4. Inspired — Marty Cagan — 2008 (2nd ed. 2017) ✅ OL
**Thesis:** Great product companies organize empowered product teams around outcomes and continuous discovery, not feature roadmaps.
- Run weekly discovery with real users (interviews + prototypes) before committing engineering time.
- Define success as a business outcome metric, not shipped features; kill ideas that don't move it.
- Keep product managers accountable for value + viability + usability + feasibility, not backlog grooming.

## 5. Lean Analytics — Alistair Croll & Benjamin Yoskovitz — 2013 ✅ OL
**Thesis:** Every startup stage has exactly One Metric That Matters; measure it, move it, then move on.
- Pick your OMTM by lifecycle stage (empathy → stickiness → virality → revenue → scale) and review it weekly.
- Cohort retention and engagement before chasing top-of-funnel growth — leaky buckets waste acquisition.
- Draw a line in the sand (target + timeframe) for every experiment so results force decisions.

## 6. High Growth Handbook — Elad Gil — 2018 ✅ OL
**Thesis:** Scaling from 10 to 10,000 people breaks everything; the CEO's job is hiring, org design, and not breaking what works.
- Hire executives for the next 12–18 months of scale, not the current size; late exec hires compound damage.
- Preserve product velocity during hypergrowth by keeping teams small and decision rights clear.
- Raise with optionality in mind: growth capital buys time to find repeatable distribution.

## 7. Traction — Gabriel Weinberg & Justin Mares — 2014 (updated eds. 2015) ✅ OL
**Thesis:** Startups fail from lack of distribution, not product; systematically test 19 traction channels to find your one.
- Run cheap parallel tests across multiple channels, then double down only on the one with real CAC:LTV economics.
- Target a single traction channel at a time once signal appears; ignore the other 18 until it saturates.
- Set critical-mass traction goals (not vanity milestones) and treat distribution as 50% of the work.

## 8. From Impossible to Inevitable — Aaron Ross & Jason Lemkin — 2016 ✅ widely catalogued (OL query inconclusive; publisher Wiley)
**Thesis:** Hypergrowth SaaS comes from nailing a niche, building repeatable sales, and layering growth levers in order.
- Nail a Niche: dominate one ICP/use case with repeatable wins before expanding segments.
- Build a sales machine with specialized roles (SDR/AE/CS) instead of heroic generalists.
- Add growth levers sequentially (niche → repeatable engine → adjacent markets → acquisitions), never all at once.

## 9. Predictable Revenue — Aaron Ross & Marylou Tyler — 2011 ✅ OL
**Thesis:** Outbound prospecting becomes predictable when you specialize roles and systematize cold outreach (lessons from Salesforce's $100M engine).
- Split prospectors (outbound SDRs) from closers (AEs) so each role optimizes one conversion rate.
- Build targeted prospect lists by ICP fit signals and run sequenced cold-email campaigns at volume.
- Track reply→meeting→opportunity conversion per SDR weekly; coach the funnel math, not gut feel.

## 10. Crossing the Chasm — Geoffrey A. Moore — 1991 (3rd ed. 2014) ✅ OL
**Thesis:** B2B technologies die in the gap between early adopters and the pragmatic majority; cross it by dominating one beachhead niche.
- Pick a single beachhead segment with a compelling reason to buy and win it completely (whole product + references).
- Sell the whole product (product + services + integrations + ecosystem), not the core tech.
- Use bowling-pin strategy: each won niche becomes the reference that topples the adjacent one.

## 11. Customer Success — Nick Mehta, Dan Steinman & Lincoln Murphy — 2016 ✅ OL
**Thesis:** In subscription businesses, retention and expansion are the growth engine — customer success is the function that owns outcomes, not support tickets.
- Define desired outcomes per segment and instrument health scores (usage + sentiment + support) against them.
- Build tech-touch/low-touch/high-touch coverage models matched to ARR tiers.
- Run systematic QBRs and expansion plays so renewals and upsells are engineered, not accidental.

## 12. Farm Don't Hunt — Guy Nirpaz & Fernanda Pires (Totango) — 2016 ✅ OL
**Thesis:** SaaS growth must shift from hunting new logos to farming existing customers through continuous value delivery.
- Instrument customer health and journey stages in real time; trigger plays on leading indicators, not churn.
- Align every team (marketing, sales, success) around account outcomes and expansion revenue, not handoffs.
- Treat onboarding as the start of the value lifecycle: time-to-first-value predicts retention and upsell.

## 13. Obviously Awesome — April Dunford — 2019 ✅ OL
**Thesis:** Positioning is a deliberate 5-component exercise (competitive alternatives, attributes, value, segments, market category) that determines whether your product is obviously the best choice.
- Position against what customers would actually do without you (status quo/competitors), not your feature list.
- Translate unique attributes into customer value themes, then prove them with evidence per segment.
- Re-position when win rates stall: test positioning with sales calls before rewriting the website.

## 14. Escaping the Build Trap — Melissa Perri — 2018 ✅ OL (lists 2019 reprint)
**Thesis:** Companies stuck shipping features (the build trap) must reorganize around product outcomes and strategy deployment.
- Replace output roadmaps with outcome-oriented goals tied to company strategy.
- Deploy a product operations cadence: discovery → delivery → measurement in tight loops.
- Restructure around empowered product teams with clear problem ownership, not project staffing.

## 15. Continuous Discovery Habits — Teresa Torres — 2021 ✅ OL
**Thesis:** Great teams do weekly customer discovery as a habit — small, continuous触点 (touchpoints) beat big research projects.
- Interview at least one customer per week per team; rotate recruiting so it never blocks.
- Maintain an opportunity solution tree: outcomes → opportunities → solutions → experiments.
- Test assumptions with the smallest viable experiment (story map, prototype, concierge) before building.

## 16. The Mom Test — Rob Fitzpatrick — 2013 ✅ OL (lists 2014 edition)
**Thesis:** Bad customer conversations produce false positives; ask about past behavior and specifics so even your mom can't mislead you.
- Ask about concrete past actions and costs ("how did you solve this last time?") instead of opinions about your idea.
- Never pitch in a learning conversation; deflect compliments and dig for commitments (time, money, intros).
- Take bad news as data: a fast "no" from evidence beats months of polite encouragement.

## 17. Subscribed — Tien Tzuo (with Gabe Weisert) — 2018 ✅ OL
**Thesis:** The subscription economy rewards companies that sell outcomes and ongoing relationships, not units of product.
- Shift KPIs from bookings to ARR, NRR, and subscriber growth; manage the business on recurring metrics.
- Design offers around access, outcomes, and tiers that grow with customer usage (land-and-expand pricing).
- Transform operations (billing, success, product) for continuous service delivery, not one-time shipment.

## 18. Never Lose a Customer Again — Joey Coleman — 2018 ✅ OL
**Thesis:** The first 100 days decide loyalty; engineer eight sequential phases from assess to advocate so new customers never regret buying.
- Run a structured first-100-days journey (affirm the purchase early; over-communicate in week one).
- Personalize early wins: get the customer to a visible outcome within days, not months.
- Systematize advocacy: ask for reviews/referrals only after documented value moments, then reward them.

## 19. Onboarding Matters — Donna Weber — 2021 ✅ publisher (Business Expert Press) + major retailers (absent from Open Library)
**Thesis:** Onboarding is orchestrated value delivery across the whole customer journey — a dedicated function, not a welcome email series.
- Build an onboarding success plan per account: milestones, owners, and measurable value outcomes.
- Segment onboarding motions (self-serve, low-touch, high-touch) by complexity and ARR, not one-size-fits-all.
- Measure onboarding with time-to-value, milestone completion, and early health — and iterate quarterly.

## 20. The Challenger Sale — Matthew Dixon & Brent Adamson — 2011 ✅ OL
**Thesis:** Top B2B reps don't build relationships first — they teach, tailor, and take control (the Challenger profile outperforms all others).
- Lead with commercial insight that reframes the customer's problem before presenting product.
- Tailor the message to individual stakeholder motivations inside the buying committee.
- Take control of the sale: push on budget, decision process, and next steps instead of endless nurturing.

## 21. Monetizing Innovation — Madhavan Ramanujam & Georg Tacke — 2016 ✅ OL
**Thesis:** Most innovations fail commercially because pricing and willingness-to-pay are decided last; monetization design must come first.
- Have value conversations with prospects before building: quantify willingness-to-pay per segment.
- Design the product and packaging around what customers will pay for, not engineering possibilities.
- Test price, offer, and messaging iteratively like features — with real buying commitments.

## 22. Blitzscaling — Reid Hoffman & Chris Yeh — 2018 ✅ OL
**Thesis:** In winner-take-all markets, prioritize speed over efficiency — raise and burn to reach scale before competitors, managing the resulting chaos deliberately.
- Decide when blitzscaling conditions apply (network effects, distribution advantages, large addressable market) — don't blitzscale otherwise.
- Scale distribution and headcount ahead of revenue certainty once product-market fit signals appear.
- Manage the chaos with counterintuitive rules: hire "good enough" fast, tolerate broken processes, raise ahead of need.

## 23. Sprint — Jake Knapp, John Zeratsky & Braden Kowitz — 2016 ✅ OL (record conflated with older edition; canonical pub. 2016, Simon & Schuster)
**Thesis:** Any hard product question can be de-risked in five days: map, sketch, decide, prototype, test with real users.
- Start every sprint with a focused long-term goal and sprint questions to bound the problem.
- Build a realistic facade prototype in one day — gold-plate nothing, test everything.
- Interview five target users on Friday and decide Monday: kill, iterate, or build.

## 24. Hacking Growth — Sean Ellis & Morgan Brown — 2017 ✅ OL
**Thesis:** Sustainable growth comes from a cross-functional growth team running high-tempo experiments across acquisition, activation, retention, revenue, and referral.
- Form a growth team with a North Star Metric and run weekly growth meetings with a test backlog.
- Instrument the full funnel (AARRR) and attack the highest-leverage constraint first, not the trendiest channel.
- Require every experiment to have a hypothesis, minimal scope, and measurable result within weeks.

## 25. The Lean Startup — Eric Ries — 2011 ✅ OL
**Thesis:** Startups are experiments under uncertainty: build → measure → learn with MVPs and validated learning to find product-market fit without waste.
- Ship the smallest MVP that tests the core value/growth hypothesis, then measure real customer behavior.
- Use innovation accounting (baselines, cohorts, actionable metrics) instead of vanity metrics to judge progress.
- Pivot or persevere on a fixed cadence: change strategy when validated learning says the engine is stalled.
