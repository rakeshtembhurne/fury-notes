# Interpersonal Communication + Everyday Influence — 25 Books

For Rocky: shy technical founder; every personal interaction should sell. Listening, charisma, networking, conversational persuasion, body language, difficult conversations. All titles verified via Open Library API (numFound > 0).

---

## 1. How to Win Friends and Influence People — Dale Carnegie (1936)
**Thesis:** People buy you before they buy your idea — arouse eager want in others by making them feel important.
**Tactics:**
1. Use names constantly and remember personal details; follow up on them next meeting.
2. Ask questions instead of giving orders ("What do you think we should try?" vs "Do X").
3. Begin with praise, call attention to mistakes indirectly, and let the other person save face.

## 2. How to Talk to Anyone — Leil Lowndes (2003)
**Thesis:** Likability is a set of 92 learnable micro-behaviors, not a personality trait.
**Tactics:**
1. "Flooding smile": pause, look at the person, then let a big warm smile flood your face.
2. "Sticky eyes": hold eye contact a beat longer than comfortable to signal intense interest.
3. Never use empty "nice to meet you" — parrot their words back ("Java? How do you handle...") to keep them talking.

## 3. Crucial Conversations — Patterson, Grenny, McMillan, Switzler (2002)
**Thesis:** High-stakes, emotional, opposing-opinion conversations succeed only when both sides feel safe.
**Tactics:**
1. Start with heart: state what you really want for yourself, others, and the relationship before speaking.
2. Use STATE: Share facts, Tell your story, Ask for their path, Talk tentatively, Encourage testing.
3. When safety breaks, step out: Contrast ("I don't want X, I do want Y"), then re-engage.

## 4. Difficult Conversations — Stone, Patton & Heen, Harvard Negotiation Project (1999)
**Thesis:** Every hard talk is three hidden conversations: facts, feelings, and identity.
**Tactics:**
1. Shift from blame to contribution: map what each side did to cause the problem.
2. Name feelings explicitly and get curious about theirs before problem-solving.
3. Ground identity threats: separate "I made an error" from "I am incompetent."

## 5. Never Split the Difference — Chris Voss (2016)
**Thesis:** FBI hostage-negotiation tactics (tactical empathy, calibrated questions) win everyday deals.
**Tactics:**
1. Label emotions ("It seems like you're worried about timeline risk") then go silent.
2. Ask calibrated how/what questions ("How am I supposed to do that?") to make them solve your problem.
3. Use the Ackerman model: 65/85/95/100% offers with empathy and a non-round final number.

## 6. The Like Switch — Jack Schafer & Marvin Karlins (2015)
**Thesis:** An ex-FBI agent's friendship formula — proximity, frequency, duration, intensity — makes anyone like you.
**Tactics:**
1. Flash an eyebrow raise + head tilt + genuine smile on first eye contact (approachability signals).
2. Use "curiosity hooks": ask open questions about their interests, then slowly link them to you.
3. Deploy empathetic statements ("You seem..." presumptive observations) instead of direct questions to lower defenses.

## 7. Captivate — Vanessa Van Edwards (2017)
**Thesis:** Charisma is decoded from predictable social cues you can learn to send and read in any interaction.
**Tactics:**
1. Launch conversations with novel sparks ("What's keeping you busy outside of work?") not "What do you do?"
2. Use the "highlighter method": hand-gesture your key words so people remember them.
3. Nodles and slow triple nods to keep people talking and signal warmth + competence.

## 8. Cues — Vanessa Van Edwards (2022)
**Thesis:** Mastering 200+ verbal and nonverbal cues lets you control the warmth-competence balance people judge you on.
**Tactics:**
1. Lead with warmth cues (open palms, leaning in, vocal warmth) before competence cues.
2. Remove danger-zone cues: crossed arms, phone on table, uptalk, filler words.
3. Mirror the other person's vocal cadence and energy level within 30 seconds of meeting.

## 9. What Every BODY Is Saying — Joe Navarro (2008)
**Thesis:** An ex-FBI agent shows the limbic brain leaks true comfort/discomfort through the feet, torso, and hands first.
**Tactics:**
1. Read feet first: pointed feet = interest, feet toward exit = wants to leave.
2. Watch for pacifying behaviors (neck touch, face rub) as real-time discomfort signals; pause and adjust.
3. Establish a baseline before judging any "tell" — deviation, not gesture, is the signal.

## 10. The Definitive Book of Body Language — Allan & Barbara Pease (2004)
**Thesis:** 60–80% of face-to-face impact is body language, and most gestures cluster into readable attitudes.
**Tactics:**
1. Never cross arms/legs when persuading; keep palms visible to project openness.
2. Match the other person's body angle, then gradually open yours to lead them.
3. Steeple, lean in, and hold eye contact in clusters to project confident authority.

## 11. Just Listen — Mark Goulston (2009)
**Thesis:** A psychiatrist's toolkit for getting through to defensive, hostile, or shut-down people via deep listening.
**Tactics:**
1. "Mirroring + labeling": repeat last 1–3 words, then name the feeling underneath.
2. Ask "magic paradox" questions: "What would need to happen for you to feel heard here?"
3. Move people from "Oh F#" through "Oh God" to "Oh well" to "OK" before asking for any decision.

## 12. You're Not Listening — Kate Murphy (2020)
**Thesis:** We have forgotten how to listen; real listening is active curiosity that keeps the speaker's story, not yours, center stage.
**Tactics:**
1. Ask one genuinely curious follow-up instead of inserting your own anecdote.
2. Treat every conversation as a chance to learn something; assume you know nothing.
3. Tolerate silence — don't fill pauses; they pull out the real answer.

## 13. The Lost Art of Listening — Michael P. Nichols (1995/2009)
**Thesis:** Listening fails not from technique gaps but from filters: criticism, rebuttal rehearsal, and unexamined hurt.
**Tactics:**
1. Listen for the request inside every complaint ("You're never home" → "I miss you").
2. Suspend rebuttal: summarize their point to their satisfaction before responding.
3. Ask "Is there more?" until the emotional charge drains, then solve.

## 14. Conversational Intelligence — Judith E. Glaser (2013)
**Thesis:** Conversations are neurochemical events: trust conversations release oxytocin, fear ones release cortisol.
**Tactics:**
1. Replace telling/selling with asking/discovering: aim for 70% questions, 30% statements.
2. Prime with "What would success look like for you?" to co-create instead of convince.
3. End every meeting naming shared success and next steps to lock in trust chemistry.

## 15. Supercommunicators — Charles Duhigg (2024)
**Thesis:** Great conversationalists match the kind of conversation (practical, emotional, social) and ask deep questions that invite vulnerability.
**Tactics:**
1. Identify the conversation type first; match feelings with feelings, facts with facts.
2. Ask looping deep questions: "How did that feel?" then reflect back what you heard.
3. Share something slightly vulnerable first to give permission for honesty.

## 16. Talk: The Science of Conversation — Elizabeth Stokoe (2018)
**Thesis:** Conversation analysis of real transcripts reveals tiny word choices (not charisma) that get yeses, defuse conflict, and move people.
**Tactics:**
1. Ask "Would you be willing to...?" instead of "Can you...?" — willingness beats ability.
2. Replace "someone will..." with named next actions; vague agency kills follow-through.
3. When someone resists, don't push harder — ask what would make it easier and summarize their words.

## 17. The Fine Art of Small Talk — Debra Fine (2005)
**Thesis:** Small talk is a learnable skill: prepared icebreakers + graceful exits turn strangers into contacts.
**Tactics:**
1. Prepare 3 go-to openers tied to the event ("What brought you here tonight?").
2. Free-information listening: pick a thread from their answer and pull it ("You mentioned Patagonia — hike or climb?").
3. Exit gracefully: summarize + business card + "great meeting you, enjoy the evening."

## 18. Never Eat Alone — Keith Ferrazzi (2005)
**Thesis:** Networking is generous relationship farming: help others first and ping constantly to stay top of mind.
**Tactics:**
1. Never eat alone: schedule one relationship-building meal/coffee per week.
2. Follow up within 24 hours of meeting anyone, with one concrete offer of help.
3. Keep a relationship action list; ping your top 20 contacts monthly with useful content.

## 19. The Charisma Myth — Olivia Fox Cabane (2012)
**Thesis:** Charisma = presence + power + warmth, and each is trainable through body language and mindset.
**Tactics:**
1. Presence drill: notice 3 physical sensations when your mind drifts mid-conversation.
2. Project power via relaxed posture, paused speech, and reduced nodding.
3. Project warmth via genuine goodwill visualization before entering any room.

## 20. Presence — Amy Cuddy (2015)
**Thesis:** Expansive posture and self-nudges change how you feel, which changes how others read you in high-stakes moments.
**Tactics:**
1. Do a 2-minute power pose privately before pitches, interviews, demos.
2. Reframe anxiety as excitement ("I'm excited") to channel arousal productively.
3. Focus on the message, not impression management — presence follows engagement.

## 21. Exactly What to Say — Phil M. Jones (2017)
**Thesis:** Tiny "magic words" subconsciously lower resistance and move everyday conversations toward yes.
**Tactics:**
1. "Just imagine..." to help prospects visualize ownership before deciding.
2. "What do you know?" as a neutral opener that avoids triggering defensiveness.
3. "How would you feel if...?" + "What happens next is..." to close without pressure.

## 22. Getting to Yes — Fisher & Ury (1981)
**Thesis:** Principled negotiation: separate people from the problem, focus on interests not positions, invent options, use objective criteria.
**Tactics:**
1. Ask "why?" behind every position to surface the real interest.
2. Brainstorm 3+ options before evaluating any of them.
3. Anchor on objective standards (market rate, precedent, expert opinion) not willpower.

## 23. Getting Past No — William Ury (1991)
**Thesis:** When the other side won't cooperate, break through stone walls and deflect attacks with a five-step strategy.
**Tactics:**
1. Go to the balcony: pause and name the game instead of reacting to attacks.
2. Disarm with an unexpected concession or acknowledgment to break the attack loop.
3. Build them a golden bridge: draft the agreement so saying yes is easy and face-saving.

## 24. Influence Without Authority — Allan R. Cohen & David L. Bradford (2005; orig. 1990)
**Thesis:** You can move peers, bosses, and partners over whom you have no power via currencies of exchange.
**Tactics:**
1. Diagnose their world: what pressures, goals, and currencies (recognition, resources, autonomy) matter to them?
2. Offer trades in their currency, not yours — build a menu of exchanges before you ask.
3. Build the relationship bank account first; influence withdrawals require prior deposits.

## 25. Fierce Conversations — Susan Scott (2002)
**Thesis:** Careers, companies, and relationships succeed or fail one honest conversation at a time — interrogate reality and resolve it.
**Tactics:**
1. Open with a 60-second "stump speech": issue, stakes, your contribution, your hope for resolution.
2. Mineral-rights questions: drill 7 layers deep ("What else? ... What's the real issue here?").
3. End every fierce conversation with a written who-does-what-by-when.
