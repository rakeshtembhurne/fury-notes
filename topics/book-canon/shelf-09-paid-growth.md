# Paid Acquisition + Growth — 25-Book Canon

Audience: technical founder running Meta ads for fashion e-commerce in India. Each entry: thesis + 3 actionable tactics.
All titles verified via Open Library API (Sep 2026).

## 1. Traffic Secrets — Russell Brunson (2020)
Thesis: You don't have followers; fill and segment your own funnels by fishing where dream customers already congregate.
- Build a "Dream 100" list of audiences/influencers and buy/earn placement inside them before scaling cold traffic.
- Segment every funnel by temperature (cold/warm/hot) with distinct hooks per segment.
- Reallocate budget weekly to the single best-converting traffic stream (fill your funnel first, then diversify).

## 2. DotCom Secrets — Russell Brunson (2015)
Thesis: Growth comes from engineered value ladders and funnels, not prettier websites.
- Structure every offer as value ladder: bait → frontend → upsell → continuity.
- Use a single conversion goal per page (squeeze, sales, or OTO) — kill nav links.
- Add order bumps and one-click upsells to raise AOV so you can outbid competitors on CAC.

## 3. Expert Secrets — Russell Brunson (2017)
Thesis: Turn expertise into a mass movement with a charismatic cause, then monetize via webinars and high-ticket offers.
- Craft an origin/epiphany story for every hero product; lead ads and advertorials with it.
- Run a "perfect webinar" arc (secret #1–3, stack, close) for high-AOV fashion bundles/collections.
- Build a free community (WhatsApp/Instagram broadcast) as the movement hub feeding launches.

## 4. $100M Leads — Alex Hormozi (2023)
Thesis: Leads are a math problem: more volume × more value per lead, driven by irresistible lead magnets and relentless outreach.
- Deploy the 4-part lead magnet (problem → solution → mechanism → promise) as quiz/style-finder for apparel.
- Run "100 outreach a day" testing matrix: 4 channels × 4 offers, kill losers weekly.
- Price/discount testing ladder: raise perceived value (bonuses, guarantees) before discounting.

## 5. $100M Offers — Alex Hormozi (2021)
Thesis: Make offers so good people feel stupid saying no by maximizing dream outcome and certainty while minimizing time and effort.
- Score every offer on the value equation; add bonuses that specifically cut time-to-result (e.g., free express alteration/shipping).
- Add risk reversal (e.g., 30-day size-exchange guarantee) to PDPs and ad copy.
- Name and stack the offer ("Monsoon Wardrobe Bundle: 3 kurtis + styling call + free exchange") instead of discounting SKUs.

## 6. Breakthrough Advertising — Eugene M. Schwartz (1966)
Thesis: Don't create desire — channel existing mass desire through stages of awareness with stage-matched headlines.
- Map every ad angle to one awareness stage (unaware → most aware); run separate creatives per stage.
- Intensify (don't invent) desire: amplify the #1 existing fashion want (e.g., festive-ready look) in hooks.
- Graduate claims: start with believable specifics before escalating promises to avoid disbelief.

## 7. Scientific Advertising — Claude C. Hopkins (1923)
Thesis: Advertising is salesmanship multiplied; test everything and let measured sales, not opinions, decide.
- Test one variable at a time (headline, offer, image) with coupon/UTM-coded ads.
- Sell the service/outcome (fit, drape, comfort), not the brand's cleverness.
- Sample smartly: free swatch/₹99 trial-tee to acquire e-mails before pitching full collections.

## 8. Ogilvy on Advertising — David Ogilvy (1983)
Thesis: Research-driven creative that respects the consumer outsells cleverness; the headline does most of the work.
- Spend disproportionate time on headlines/hooks: test 5+ per creative (benefit + news angle wins).
- Use long copy and specifics (prices, fabric GSM, wash counts) when the product justifies it.
- Put the brand name, promise, and offer in the first 3 seconds of every video ad.

## 9. Traction — Gabriel Weinberg & Justin Mares (2015)
Thesis: Startups fail from lack of distribution, not product; systematically test 19 channels to find your one scalable engine.
- Run parallel 2-week "traction tests" with a fixed budget across 3–4 channels (Meta, Google Shopping, affiliates, marketplaces).
- Define one traction goal (e.g., CAC < ₹450) per test; only scale channels that hit it.
- Layer an "unscalable" channel (pop-ups, campus reps, WhatsApp groups) to bootstrap the scalable one.

## 10. Hacking Growth — Sean Ellis & Morgan Brown (2017)
Thesis: Growth is a cross-functional, high-tempo testing machine (analyze → ideate → prioritize → test), not hacks.
- Run a weekly growth sprint: backlog ideas, score ICE (impact/confidence/ease), test top 3.
- Instrument one North Star metric (e.g., repeat purchase rate) plus funnel dashboards before spending more.
- Require every test to have a hypothesis, sample size, and kill/scale rule declared upfront.

## 11. Growth Hacker Marketing — Ryan Holiday (2013)
Thesis: Fit the product to a market, find a growth lever that scales, and let that lever be the marketing.
- Start with product-market fit surveys ("very disappointed" ≥40%) before scaling Meta budgets.
- Launch to a tight niche (e.g., plus-size festive wear) and engineer shareability (referral credit, UGC contests).
- Turn one scalable tactic (e.g., influencer seeding → whitelisted Spark-style ads) into the whole engine.

## 12. Lean Analytics — Alistair Croll & Benjamin Yoskovitz (2013)
Thesis: Pick the One Metric That Matters for your current stage and ignore vanity metrics.
- Define stage-gated OMTM: empathy → stickiness → virality → revenue → scale (e.g., today: checkout conversion).
- Cohort every campaign report (new vs returning, size curve, city tier) instead of blended ROAS.
- Set explicit "normal" baselines so tests are judged against reality, not hope.

## 13. High Growth Handbook — Elad Gil (2018)
Thesis: Scaling is a different job: hire the right leaders late, raise smart, and manage the board while growth compounds.
- Hire "slope, not y-intercept" growth marketers; give them P&L and kill/scale authority.
- Raise/decide ad budgets on payback windows (e.g., 60-day CAC recovery), not last-click ROAS snapshots.
- Expand only after repeatable playbook: one winning creative system × one winning audience × one winning offer.

## 14. Blitzscaling — Reid Hoffman & Chris Yeh (2018)
Thesis: When network effects and speed decide winners, prioritize speed over efficiency — raise and burn to capture the market.
- Identify if/when a blitz is warranted (festive season share grab) vs disciplined growth the rest of the year.
- Remove growth blockers (COD limits, size-exchange friction, stockouts) before pouring fuel on ads.
- Sequence blitzes: city-by-city or category-by-category, not everything at once.

## 15. Hooked — Nir Eyal (2014)
Thesis: Retention is engineered through habit loops: trigger → action → variable reward → investment.
- Attach external triggers (abandoned-cart WhatsApp, price-drop push) to internal triggers (festive anxiety, fit worry).
- Add variable rewards to retention flows (mystery coupon, early-access drops) to build checking habits.
- Increase investment mechanics (style profiles, wishlists, fit data) so switching costs rise.

## 16. Never Lose a Customer Again — Joey Coleman (2018)
Thesis: The first 100 days decide loyalty; engineer the eight phases from assess to advocate to stop churn.
- Build a 100-day onboarding: confirm purchase → fit-guide call → style content → first-repurchase nudge.
- Celebrate the "accomplish" moment (first compliment on the outfit — solicit and reshare UGC).
- Turn exchanges/returns into advocacy moments with prepaid, instant, no-questions swaps.

## 17. You Should Test That — Chris Goward (2012)
Thesis: Conversion lifts come from a disciplined LIFT-model testing program, not redesigns.
- Prioritize tests by LIFT six factors (value prop, relevance, clarity, anxiety, distraction, urgency).
- Always test value proposition first (offer framing), then layout — never the reverse.
- Document every test as case study (hypothesis, result, insight) to compound learnings across PDPs.

## 18. Landing Page Optimization — Tim Ash (2008; 2nd ed. 2012)
Thesis: Landing pages convert when message match, persuasion architecture, and friction removal align.
- Match headline + hero image exactly to the ad creative (same kurti, same offer, same price).
- Cut form friction: guest checkout, PIN-code-first, UPI/COD prominent, autofill on.
- Add trust clusters near CTA: reviews with photos, exchange policy, COD badge, delivery date.

## 19. Don't Make Me Think — Steve Krug (2000; 3rd ed. 2014)
Thesis: Usability wins when pages are self-evident; remove question marks and let users satisfice.
- Make the PDP answer in 5 seconds: price, sizes in stock, delivery date, exchange terms.
- One dominant CTA per viewport; eliminate competing banners near "Add to Bag."
- Usability-test weekly with 3 users on mobile (the dominant fashion traffic source in India).

## 20. Ca$hvertising — Drew Eric Whitman (2008)
Thesis: Buying behavior is driven by 8 hardwired life forces plus proven ad-psych triggers — write to them.
- Lead every ad with one of the 8 desires (survival/enjoyment of life, sexual companionship, care of loved ones, social approval…).
- Use the "greed/fear/exclusivity" triad in sale copy (limited festive stock, price-rise warning).
- Apply the 17 consumer-psych triggers (contrast pricing, specificity, story) to PDP bullets and ad primary text.

## 21. The 1-Page Marketing Plan — Allan Dib (2016)
Thesis: All marketing fits on one page: target → message → media → capture → nurture → convert → deliver → upsell → referrals.
- Define a single avatar + single message-market match before launching any campaign.
- Build capture (lead magnet/quiz) and nurture (email/SMS/WhatsApp) before scaling cold spend.
- Allocate budget by lifetime value tiers: higher LTV segments get higher target CAC.

## 22. Ultimate Guide to Google Ads — Perry Marshall, Mike Rhodes & Bryan Todd (6th ed. 2020; series since 2004)
Thesis: Google rewards relevance and intent-matching; win with tight ad groups, Swiss-army-knife campaigns, and relentless negative keywords.
- Structure Shopping + Performance Max with segmented asset groups per margin tier (hero SKUs vs long tail).
- Mine search terms weekly; promote winners to exact match, negate tire-kickers.
- Test RLSA + branded defense so competitors don't poach high-intent festive queries.

## 23. The Ultimate Guide to Facebook Advertising — Perry Marshall, Keith Krance & Thomas Meloche (3rd ed. 2017; series since 2011)
Thesis: Facebook wins come from audience-message match plus systematic creative testing inside a simple account structure.
- Simplify structure (broad + 1–2 interest stacks + retargeting) and let creative do the targeting.
- Test creatives in batches (5 hooks × 2 formats weekly); graduate winners to scale campaigns.
- Use social proof stacking: seed engagement, pin best comments, whitelist creator content as ads.

## 24. Email Marketing Rules — Chad S. White (1st ed. 2013; 3rd ed. 2017)
Thesis: Subscriber-centric, lifecycle-driven email (not batch blasts) drives revenue: right message, person, time.
- Build core lifecycle flows first: welcome → abandon (browse/cart/checkout) → post-purchase → winback.
- Segment by recency/frequency/monetary + category affinity; suppress recent purchasers from discount blasts.
- Optimize deliverability basics: sunset inactives, authenticate (SPF/DKIM/DMARC), prefer plain-lean hybrid templates on mobile.

## 25. Predictable Revenue — Aaron Ross & Marylou Tyler (2011)
Thesis: Predictable pipelines come from specialized prospecting (cold outbound + inbound) with dedicated roles and cadences.
- Separate hunters (prospecting wholesale/affiliate partners) from closers and success roles.
- Run multi-touch outbound cadences (email → call → LinkedIn/WhatsApp) for B2B lines (corporate gifting, reseller partnerships).
- Track conversion by stage (reply → meeting → deal) and optimize the bottleneck stage, not the whole funnel at once.

## Bonus cross-cut for fashion e-commerce (India)
- Ecommerce Evolved — Tanner Larsson (2016): build a real brand (story, continuity/subscription, backend ascension) instead of living deal-to-deal.
- Expert-community companion: Ecommerce Email/SMS flows + COD confirmation on WhatsApp routinely beat pure ad tweaks for net ROAS.
