# Global / Emerging-Market Selling + Cross-Cultural Business — 25-Book Canon

For Rocky: Indian founder selling to US + India. Verified titles.

| # | Title | Author | Year | Thesis | Tactic 1 | Tactic 2 | Tactic 3 |
|---|-------|--------|------|--------|----------|----------|----------|
| 1 | The Culture Map | Erin Meyer | 2014 | Map 8 behavior scales (feedback, trust, decisions) to decode cross-cultural friction. | Plot US vs India on each scale before negotiations | Give US buyers direct negative feedback, Indians indirect with wrap | Build trust task-first for US, relationship-first for India |
| 2 | Kiss, Bow, or Shake Hands | Terri Morrison & Wayne A. Conaway | 2006 | Country-by-country field guide to protocol, negotiation, and etiquette. | Pull the 5-page brief for your buyer's country before every call | Mirror greeting, card, and seating protocol on first meetings | Adapt pitch pace: fast-close US, slow-consensus Asia |
| 3 | When Cultures Collide | Richard D. Lewis | 1996 | Classifies cultures as linear-active, multi-active, reactive to predict business behavior. | Classify each prospect org into Lewis's 3 types | Use data-first decks for linear-active US/Germany, story-first for multi-active | Let reactive cultures (Japan, SE Asia) pause — don't fill silence |
| 4 | Riding the Waves of Culture | Fons Trompenaars & Charles Hampden-Turner | 1993 | 7 value dilemmas (universalism vs particularism, etc.) explain cross-border conflict. | Diagnose which dilemma is blocking a stalled deal | Write universal rules for US contracts, allow particularist exceptions locally | Reconcile, don't pick sides: sell standardization + local flex together |
| 5 | Global Dexterity | Andy Molinsky | 2013 | Code-switch behavior without losing authenticity to work across cultures. | Rehearse one stretch behavior per week (e.g., direct US pushback) | Customize: keep your accent/identity, adapt turn-taking and assertiveness | Debrief every cross-cultural call: what felt unnatural, what worked |
| 6 | The Fortune at the Bottom of the Pyramid | C. K. Prahalad | 2004 | 4B low-income consumers are a profitable market if you redesign price and access. | Design single-serve / sachet pricing for India trials | Partner with local distribution (kirana, SHGs) instead of D2C only | Co-create with users; strip features to hit affordability threshold |
| 7 | Breakout Nations | Ruchir Sharma | 2012 | Emerging markets diverge — back only breakout nations with reform + discipline. | Score markets on Sharma's rules (debt, inflation, leadership) before expansion | Avoid herd BRICS hype; pick 2–3 breakout bets | Re-check class: booms end when billionaires-from-connections spike |
| 8 | The Rise and Fall of Nations | Ruchir Sharma | 2016 | 10 rules (50/5, second cities, factories) predict which economies will rise next. | Track cover-story curse: exit markets on magazine-cover euphoria | Favor second cities over capitals for hiring and pilots | Watch factory wages and manufacturing share as expansion signals |
| 9 | Poor Economics | Abhijit Banerjee & Esther Duflo | 2011 | RCT evidence shows the poor decide rationally under constraints — design for that. | Run micro-RCTs on pricing/messages instead of surveys | Remove small frictions (forms, travel, defaults) to lift uptake | Price for irregular cash flows: micropayments, pay-as-you-go |
| 10 | The $10 Trillion Prize | Michael J. Silverstein et al. | 2012 | China's and India's rising middle class is a $10T consumer prize this decade. | Segment by aspiration level, not just income | Win with value + status: affordable premium beats cheapest | Localize product, brand story, and service for each city's tier |
| 11 | Globality | Hal Sirkin, Jim Hemerling & Arindam Bhattacharya | 2008 | Challenger firms from emerging markets beat incumbents by competing in every market. | Attack incumbents' high-margin home (US) while defending India base | Use low-cost engineering in India to fund US go-to-market | Hire local US faces early; don't sell remote-only |
| 12 | The Polyester Prince | Hamish McDonald | 1998 | Unauthorized Dhirubhai Ambani bio: how Reliance scaled via policy, markets, and audacity. | Study how Reliance turned regulation into moat — map your own policy edge | Build retail-investor trust with simple equity narratives | Scale with backward integration: own more of the value chain |
| 13 | Ambani & Sons | Hamish McDonald | 2010 | Sequel on the Ambani split; succession and governance make or break empires. | Write a founder succession pact before you need one | Separate family and cap-table governance in writing | Keep political diversification: never depend on one patron |
| 14 | The Z Factor | Subhash Chandra | 2016 | Zee/Essel founder memoir: outsider persistence beats pedigree in Indian business. | Sell first, build later: pre-sell distribution before capacity | Bet on under-served languages/regions competitors ignore | Survive debt cycles by selling non-core assets fast |
| 15 | The Elephant and the Dragon | Robyn Meredith | 2007 | India (services/chaotic democracy) vs China (manufacturing/mandarin state) growth models. | Choose: China playbook for scale manufacturing, India for frugal services | Price for Indian jugaad: modular, repairable, low-maintenance | Use diaspora bridges to open US doors from India |
| 16 | The World Is Flat | Thomas L. Friedman | 2005 | Flatteners (outsourcing, open source, supply chains) let small firms sell globally. | Outsource non-core to India, keep US-facing sales/support in US time zones | Productize a global supply chain story in your pitch | Use flat-world tools (cloud, freelance, VoIP) to look big from day one |
| 17 | One Billion Customers | James McGregor | 2005 | Insider lessons on selling into China: relationships plus contracts, never either alone. | Pair every China/Asia deal with a local fixer plus a written contract | Never rely on one relationship; map the committee behind the signer | Localize compliance from day one; don't retrofit |
| 18 | Cracking the Emerging Markets Enigma | G. Andrew Karolyi | 2015 | Quantify home bias and risk to price emerging-market risk correctly. | Score country risk into pricing, not gut feel | Diversify across 3+ emerging markets to smooth volatility | Disclose risks explicitly to US buyers to build credibility |
| 19 | Reverse Innovation | Vijay Govindarajan & Chris Trimble | 2012 | Innovate cheap in emerging markets first, then carry breakthroughs back to the US. | Launch stripped-down MVP in India, port winners to US | Set up separate India P&L so corporate metrics don't kill frugal bets | Clean-slate design: ban "just remove features" — rebuild for cost |
| 20 | The New Multinationals | Mauro Guillén & Esteban García-Canal | 2010 | Emerging-market multinationals win via acquisition, networks, and accelerated learning. | Buy a tiny US customer base instead of cold-starting | Use alliances and diaspora networks to shortcut credibility | Codify every market entry into a repeatable playbook |
| 21 | Selling to Big Companies | Jill Konrath | 2005 | Crack US enterprise: short, sharp value touches that respect busy buyers. | Open with a 30-second value trigger tied to their 10-K pain | Multi-thread 5+ stakeholders per US account | End every touch with one low-friction next step |
| 22 | The Challenger Sale | Matthew Dixon & Brent Adamson | 2011 | Top US reps teach, tailor, and take control instead of relationship-building. | Teach an insight about their business before pitching product | Tailor the ROI story per stakeholder (CFO vs end user) | Take control: push back politely and propose a decision timeline |
| 23 | Stay Hungry Stay Foolish | Rashmi Bansal | 2008 | 25 IIM-A grads turned entrepreneurs: Indian startups win without pedigree playbooks. | Interview 10 Indian customers weekly; build from field notes | Start services-first for cash flow, productize later | Use campus and alumni networks as first distribution |
| 24 | A Better India: A Better World | N. R. Narayana Murthy | 2009 | Infosys founder's lectures: values-based, global-quality business from India. | Sell "predictability + ethics" as differentiator to US buyers | Over-invest in training to offset brand disadvantage abroad | Publish values/SLA transparency to win enterprise trust |
| 25 | The Lexus and the Olive Tree | Thomas L. Friedman | 1999 | Globalization rewards speed and openness, but local roots (olive trees) still decide deals. | Frame your pitch as Lexus (global scale) + olive tree (local roots) | Move fast on tech/integration; go slow on culture and trust | Hedge currency and geopolitical risk in every cross-border contract |
